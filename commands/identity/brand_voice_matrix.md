# Brand Voice Matrix: TLDR

## 1. The Core Personality
* **Primary Archetype:** **The Trusted Curator / The Editor-in-Chief.** (Clarity, brevity, editorial judgment, service to the reader.)
* **The "Three Words":** Our voice is **Concise, Trustworthy, and Sharp**.
* **Brand Personality Traits:** Informational · Clean · Authoritative · Approachable · Efficient.

## 2. The "This, Not That" Framework
| We Are... | But We Are NOT... | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Concise & Respectful of Time** | Terse or Cold | TLDR saves people time, but we're not robotic. Brief doesn't mean unfriendly — our personality comes through in word choice, not word count. |
| **Authoritative & Trusted** | Preachy or Opinionated | We curate and inform. We don't tell readers what to think about the news. Editorial judgment is in what we select, not in how we spin it. |
| **Sharp & Slightly Wry** | Sarcastic or Edgy | A touch of personality keeps the format from feeling dry. We might say "the TL;DR of the TL;DR" — but we never mock, snark, or punch down. |
| **Professional & Clean** | Corporate or Stiff | Our audience is tech professionals, not procurement committees. We can be direct and human without sounding like a legal brief. |
| **Free & Open** | Desperate or Cheap | "Free" is a feature of our business model, not a discount. We're free because advertising supports the newsletter — not because the product isn't valuable. |

## 3. Voice by Context

### Reader-Facing Contexts

* **Newsletter content:** **Informational & Efficient.** "GitHub was compromised via an AI triage bot in a supply chain attack affecting 4,000 machines." — News first, context second, no throat-clearing.
* **Signup pages & landing pages:** **Welcoming & Clear.** "Keep up with tech in 5 minutes. Get the free daily email with summaries of the most interesting stories in startups, tech, and programming."
* **Subscriber acquisition ads:** **Confident & Benefit-Led.** "1,600,000+ tech pros start their day with TLDR. 5 minutes. Free." — Social proof + value proposition + no friction.
* **Email onboarding:** **Warm & Helpful.** "Welcome to TLDR. Tomorrow morning you'll get your first issue. It takes about 5 minutes to read. We think you'll like it."
* **Social media (organic):** **Casual & Topical.** Share interesting stories with brief commentary. React to tech news in real-time. Be part of the conversation, not above it.

### Advertiser-Facing Contexts

* **Sales pages (advertise.tldr.tech):** **Professional & Data-Driven.** "Reach over 6 million tech professionals. Copywriting services and campaign performance reports included at no additional cost." — Lead with the audience, prove the value, remove objections.
* **Case studies:** **Evidence-Based & Specific.** "Delve drove $1M in attributed pipeline — 52x ROI — through TLDR ads." — Let the numbers speak. Real companies, real results, real quotes.
* **Outbound sales emails:** **Direct & Relevant.** "Your audience reads TLDR. Here's how [similar company] generated $382K in pipeline at 20x ROI." — Three sentences. One case study. One CTA.
* **Advertiser onboarding:** **Supportive & Consultative.** "Here's how we'll work together: our team writes your ad copy to match our editorial voice, you review and approve, and we handle the rest."

## 4. Vocabulary & Diction

### Words We Use
* "Curated," "Briefing," "Signal" (reader context)
* "5 minutes," "Free," "Join [X] readers" (growth context)
* "Pipeline," "ROI," "CPC," "Open rate," "Native" (advertiser context)
* "Newsletter," "Daily email," "Tech professionals" (universal)

### Words We Avoid
* "Content" (when we mean "news" or "stories" — too generic)
* "Blast" (implies spam; we "send" or "deliver" newsletters)
* "Cheap" (we're "cost-effective" or "lower CPC" — never cheap)
* "Users" (they're "readers" or "subscribers" on the reader side; "advertisers" or "sponsors" on the revenue side)
* "Guaranteed" (we share case studies and benchmarks, never guaranteed results)

## 5. Practical Writing Rules
* **Lead with the number or the news.** Not: "We're excited to announce that..." → Yes: "1,600,000+ readers. 48% open rate. 3 ads per newsletter."
* **One idea per sentence.** Compound sentences slow readers down. TLDR readers value speed — so should our copy.
* **Active voice.** Not: "The newsletter is read by millions." → Yes: "Millions read the newsletter."
* **Specificity over superlatives.** Not: "The best tech newsletter." → Yes: "The most-read daily tech newsletter — 1.6M subscribers."

## 6. The TLDR Brand in One Line
* **For readers:** "Keep up with tech in 5 minutes."
* **For advertisers:** "Reach 7M+ tech professionals where they actually pay attention."
* **For both:** "TLDR — a byte-sized daily tech newsletter."

---
### AI Agent Context Rule:
Before generating text, determine:
1. **Which side of the network?** Reader or advertiser.
2. **Which context?** (Newsletter, ad, sales page, email, social, internal)
3. **Apply the matching voice** from Section 3 above.
If the output would confuse a reader with advertiser language (or vice versa), rewrite. The two sides should never bleed into each other.
