# Numeral Distribution GTM Presentation: Speaker Notes

## Slide 1: Executive Summary & The Paradigm Shift
Good morning, everyone. Today, we're here to discuss a massive growth opportunity for Numeral: expanding into the Wholesale Distribution market. Historically, our success in the B2C retail space has been driven by an "efficiency play"—saving our customers time. But moving into wholesale requires a fundamental paradigm shift. We are no longer selling "time savings"; we are selling "risk mitigation and liability protection." 

The core of this strategy revolves around Exemption Certificates. Wholesale distributors carry immense compliance liability across multiple states simply to prove why they *didn't* collect tax. This administrative burden and financial risk represent our wedge. 

Our strategy is deliberate: by gating essential B2B features like Exemption Certificate Management and complex API integrations behind our Pro Custom tier, we systematically disqualify these distributors from our transactional pricing model. This forces a transition into a value-based, sales-led motion, allowing us to anchor our pricing against the massive financial risk of a failed state audit, drastically increasing our Average Contract Value.

## Slide 2: Target Audience & Identification
So, who exactly are we selling to? We're targeting Wholesale Distributors with over $50 Million in Annual Recurring Revenue, operating across large geographic footprints with complex supply chains. But to win these enterprise deals, we need to speak to the entire buying committee.

Our Champion is the Director of Tax & Compliance—the person feeling the daily pain of tracking these exemption certificates and dealing with manual ERP data entry.

Our Economic Buyer, and the ultimate Risk Bearer, is the CFO or VP of Finance. They hold the budget, but more importantly, they hold the legal and financial liability if a multi-state audit goes wrong. Our messaging to them is all about risk mitigation.

Finally, our Technical Evaluator is the IT Director or ERP Administrator who needs to know that our API can securely integrate with their legacy systems like NetSuite or Acumatica.

To reach them, our execution flow happens in three steps. First, we use Technographic profiling to identify companies running target B2B ERPs. Second, we apply Firmographic filtering to narrow down by NAICS codes and revenue thresholds. And finally, we perform Job Title Persona Mapping to scrape exact targets, allowing us to deliver highly relevant, role-specific messaging—focusing on "Operational Efficiency" for the Tax Director and "Financial Risk" for the CFO.

## Slide 3: Website Assessment
To execute this GTM effectively, our website needs to act as a highly targeted net. We are focusing on six key levers to align the digital experience.

First, Building an Integrations Page. We need a dedicated, SEO-optimized directory that prominently showcases our native connections to core B2B ERPs like NetSuite, Acumatica, and Sage. This will drive high-intent technical search traffic and immediately address the central needs of our Technical Evaluator right out of the gate.

Second, a complete Messaging Pivot. Our Distribution solutions page needs to abandon the retail 'time-savings' message entirely, focusing instead on financial risk, audit preparation, and liability protection.

Third, an Interactive Product Demo. To reduce friction and accelerate the sales cycle, we'll embed an interactive demo showing exactly how we manage the workflow of complex exemption certificates. 

Fourth, we will Build Growth Interactive Tools for segmentation, lead capture, and lead nurture. We are proposing five specific tools to act as high-value lead magnets. 

Tool one is the "Audit Exposure" Liability Calculator, which quantifies the hidden financial risk of missing certificates to project potential audit liabilities. The hook here offers an audit defense checklist to capture the lead.

Tool two is a Multi-State Nexus "Blindspot" Detector. Wholesalers often overlook nexus triggers like 3PLs or remote staff. We'll use a simple checklist to generate a heat map of their unguarded states, capturing their info before revealing the specific results.

Tool three is an Exemption Certificate "Rules Engine." We'll untangle state-by-state rules—like California's strict requirements—and exchange the full matrix for their contact information.

Tool four is our Drop-Ship "Tax Pyramid" Simulator. Drop-shipping tax logic is incredibly messy. We'll provide a flowchart simulator to demonstrate our API's ability to automate three-party workflows, driving them toward a quick consultation.

Finally, tool five is the Use Tax "Leakage" Estimator, which calculates the often-ignored penalties of pulling resale inventory for internal use.

These tools work for Numeral because they are highly specific—speaking the language of a VP of Finance—while capturing high-intent leads and generating tremendous SEO value for the exact long-tail queries our target personas are actively searching for.

Fifth, a Qualified Chat Experience. By deploying an Agentic SDR, we can immediately qualify complex compliance requirements right on the site, significantly reducing sales friction before a human ever gets involved.

Finally, our CTA Strategy. We are removing self-serve pricing for this ICP entirely, replacing it with a "Book a FREE Demo" CTA to drive high-value conversations.

Ultimately, these levers all drive toward one goal: pivoting from retail time-savings to B2B risk mitigation to unlock enterprise-tier ACVs.

## Slide 4: Marketing Channels & Go-To-Market Mechanics
Moving on to how we reach this audience. We are prioritizing Account-Based Marketing and highly targeted B2B Demand Generation over broad awareness.

Our channel mix is deliberate. Our highest priority is AI-Driven Outbound using platforms like Clay to orchestrate hyper-personalized email sequences targeting CFOs directly regarding audit risks. 

Equally important is our Paid Ads and Social strategy. We'll deploy a "Clay Ads Strategy," building static, highly targeted audiences of our exact Named Accounts and uploading them directly into LinkedIn and Meta to ensure zero wasted ad spend. 

Medium priorities include SEO for bottom-of-funnel queries and Partner Alliances with B2B ERP system integrators. Digital PR and Event Marketing will play a supporting role in building high-level thought leadership over time.

Creatively, we are running two main plays: The "Risk or Fear Play," visualizing the financial devastation of a failed audit, and the "Trust and Security Play," emphasizing complete coverage and unified reporting.

In the early days, our budget allocation will be aggressive to establish a foothold: 50% dedicated to outbound infrastructure and data curation for our Clay strategy, 30% to high-intent Search and LinkedIn Lead Gen, and a crucial 20% reserved as "Contract Buyout Subsidies" to proactively buy target accounts out of their existing Avalara or Vertex contracts, significantly accelerating our sales cycle.

## Slide 5: Path to Scale
As we establish our foothold, our focus will shift to scaling this motion efficiently.

First, we'll transition from manual outbound efforts to Intent-Driven Triggers. This means automatically launching ABM outreach the moment a distributor hits a new state nexus or expands a warehouse footprint.

Second, our Integration Ecosystem will become a structural moat. By building deeper, native integrations with legacy B2B ERPs, we reduce onboarding friction and make our platform indispensable.

Finally, Pricing Expansion. While keeping the Pro package Custom, we will introduce systematic volume-based pricing tiers based purely on the volume of exemption certificates managed, ensuring our revenue scales directly with the customer's operational complexity.

## Slide 6: Risk Mitigation & Challenges
Of course, moving upmarket into distribution comes with significant challenges, but we have a mitigation strategy for each.

The first risk is Integration Bottlenecks. Legacy distributors run messy, on-premise ERPs. To offset the burden on our engineering teams, we'll develop standardized middleware playbooks and partner heavily with specialized system integrators.

The second matrix is Incumbent Entrenchment. Solutions like Avalara are deeply embedded. We won't propose an immediate rip-and-replace. Instead, we'll sell our Exemption Certificate Management module as an agile wedge to get our foot in the door, paired with our aggressive Contract Buyouts to eliminate the financial friction of switching.

Third, Enterprise sales mean Long Sales Cycles. We will combat this by co-opting the Technical Evaluator early in the presales motion, providing robust security documentation and sandbox API access right out of the gate.

Lastly, Market Disconnect. We need to gracefully handle smaller wholesalers who want exemption features but fall below our Custom ACV threshold. We will implement clear routing rules on the website to filter these leads to our partner network or an automated self-serve rejection nurture track, protecting our brand sentiment while keeping our sales org focused.

## Slide 7: Sales & Marketing Symbiosis
To win in this segment, Marketing cannot operate in a silo. Our primary mandate here is Pipeline Acceleration and SDR Enablement.

Marketing and Sales will jointly agree on a strict Named Account List. Marketing will run the "Clay Engine" to enrich these accounts so SDRs are only calling into highly prioritized, data-rich targets.

We're implementing advanced lead segmentation. High-fit enterprise leads seeking exemption certificates will bypass standard SDR qualification and route directly to Senior Account Executives. Lower intent MQLs will enter specialized, long-term nurture tracks focused on multi-state audit risks.

From a handoff perspective, we are establishing strict SLAs for when an account transitions from Marketing Nurture to SDR Outreach, triggered by high-value interactions.

Post-sale, we are replacing our self-serve onboarding with a "White-Glove Implementation Plan" designed in tandem with Customer Success to accelerate time-to-value for these massive accounts.

Marketing will also arm the revenue team with tailored enablement assets: Competitor Battlecards for legacy ERP compliance, updated Positioning Guides focusing on risk mitigation over time-savings, and clear guidelines on leveraging Pro tier features to anchor high ACVs.

Finally, a bi-weekly "Pipeline Council" between Marketing and Sales will ensure a continuous feedback loop, turning frontline SDR intelligence directly into top-of-funnel ad creative.

## Slide 8: Defining Success (1H & 2026 Vision)
So, what does success look like?

In the first half of the year, our goals are highly tactical. We aim to secure 5 to 10 closed-won Distribution Enterprise accounts. These initial logos will serve to validate our custom pricing model hypothesis, establishing a baseline ACV, Customer Acquisition Cost, and sales cycle length. We will also have fully completed our essential infrastructure upgrades—visibly gating features on the website and equipping our SDR team with the new messaging tracks.

Looking further ahead to 2026, the vision is transformative. The Wholesale Distribution segment should represent a significant percentage of our total company ARR—ideally 30% or more. We want Numeral to be universally recognized not just as a retail filing tool, but as the definitive compliance standard for complex B2B wholesale. Ultimately, Exemption Certificate Management will prove to be our most undeniable, highest-margin feature for driving Enterprise upgrades.

## Slide 9: The Unasked Questions
As we close out the strategy overview, I want to leave the leadership team with two critical, overarching questions that we must continue to evaluate as we execute.

First, are we operationally equipped within our Customer Success organization to support the highly technical API and ERP onboarding that these enterprise accounts will inevitably demand? We must ensure our CS team scales alongside our sales org.

Second, does our platform natively support the unique edge-cases of wholesale dropshipping across multiple tax jurisdictions today, or will we need to commit engineering resources to build custom solutions per account?

These are the operational realities we need to align on to ensure that when we win these massive enterprise deals, we retain them successfully. Thank you, and I’m happy to take any questions.
