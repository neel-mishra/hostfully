# Mosaic Enterprise GTM Speaker Notes

Use these notes alongside `mosaic_enterprise_gtm_deck_outline.md` as the presenter script. Keep the tone operational: what we do, why it matters, and how we measure it.

---

## Slide 1: Agenda
"Today is about how Mosaic scales adoption across two audiences: individual creators (fast activation) and teams at scale (enterprise and agencies with integrations, governance, and security requirements). We'll walk through the ICP, the channel system, and the operational machinery that turns intent into meetings and meetings into onboarding. We'll then cover AI Ops, risks, and a concrete 3-month roadmap."

## Slide 2: Executive Summary & The Mosaic Mandate
"The mandate is not to sell 'video editing software'. The mandate is to sell workflow outcomes: time saved and more shipped content, with enterprise-grade governance and pipeline integrations. We run a dual engine:
1) B2C: creators adopt via templates and agentic workflows, with compounding distribution driven by sharing.
2) B2B: enterprise teams and agencies adopt by integrating Mosaic into their pipeline and automating workflows with triggers and APIs.

The flywheel is simple: B2C adoption improves proof and trust; agencies in particular can translate creator-style outcomes into client delivery. Meanwhile B2B intent is routed into demos using intent signals from enterprise and technical surfaces."

## Slide 3: Competitive Landscape & Structural Moat
"On this slide we use a three-column table — **Competitor, Focus, Mosaic advantage** — and focus only on node-based creation/editing competitors: **FLORA, Weavy, Krea (Nodes), Kaiber Superstudio, and ComfyUI**.

The key narrative is that these platforms are strong at creative generation and node-based experimentation, but Mosaic is positioned as a more complete video-operations system for teams.

Our advantage compounds where teams care about implementation and scale:
* shared workspaces and RBAC,
* centralized asset libraries and audit logs,
* SOC 2 Type II posture and no-training positioning,
* integrations with MAM/storage and NLE roundtrip exports,
* and API-driven automation with webhooks.

This means we can sell adoption and repeatability, not one-off demos."

## Slide 4: The Exhaustion of Traditional Video Ops
"Traditional editing breaks at scale because repetitive steps dominate and collaboration and governance lag behind. Mosaic inverts this:
* design once in the canvas,
* run workflows on autopilot,
* trigger pipelines by events,
* and export into existing NLE workflows.

So our marketing should emphasize operational throughput and governance readiness."

## Slide 6: Website & Digital Experience Alignment
"Our digital surfaces should match the adoption motion.

For B2C: templates, canvas workflow exploration, and content engine entry points should reduce time-to-aha. For B2B: `/enterprise` should be the trust narrative—governance, integrations, custom automations, and security/compliance. Then the API docs and automation pages are where technical evaluators decide Mosaic is implementable.

We should treat demo booking and API exploration as separate conversion states, not a single funnel.

Add a dedicated **PLG interactive tools** section on this slide with four concepts:
1) **Sandbox interactive demo (no signup)** where a lead navigates a guided UI tour, runs a workflow, and gets an AHA moment before trial commitment.
2) **Workflow ROI calculator** that translates team inputs into time saved and output impact.
3) **Template-to-outcome wizard** that maps use case to recommended template stack and expected result.
4) **Integration readiness checker** that scores stack fit (MAM/NLE/security) and outputs implementation steps.

These tools give us higher-intent product-qualified signals for ABM routing and sales handoff."

## Slide 7: Marketing Channels & Go-To-Market Mechanics (B2C + B2B split)
"This is a multi-channel system that follows the YC growth internship constraints: `paid, content, community, partnerships`, and we run experiments across those channels.

For B2C creators: we use template-first PLG, outcome-driven paid acquisition, workflow teaching content (SEO and how-to proof), and community/partnership trust transfer.

For B2B: we run AI ABM outbound and dark funnel intent triggers, then we scale through role-targeted paid social and partnerships. The key change from a 'generic enterprise' plan is that agencies are treated as a separate buyer motion in B2B. Their creative leadership and operational constraints are different, and the routing should reflect that.

The success metric is not only demos booked. It's demo-to-technical-onboarding conversion."

## Slide 8: Referral Mechanisms & Professional Virality
"Referrals for professionals are status-safe and utility-driven. For creators, referrals should feel like they are sharing a workflow advantage or a portfolio-ready output. For agencies, referrals should map to scalable client delivery: shareable template packs and workspace governance.

This is also where we introduce a GitHub-like creator community:
* creators publish open workflows,
* others fork/remix and improve them,
* improvements are re-published into the shared network.

We also turn those workflow runs into **UGC proof videos**:
* creators post short workflow clips and outcomes on LinkedIn and Instagram,
* each post links back to the workflow/template so other creators can fork/remix.

That creates the network effect we want: each new creator increases the utility of the network for everyone else, which improves activation and attracts more creators in a compounding flywheel.

To prevent referral fraud, we credit referrals only when adoption signals show real workflow usage—enough to create value, not empty sign-ups."

## Slide 9: White-Glove Workflow Implementation (Mosaic version)
"In enterprise and agencies, Mosaic must feel implementable on day one. The white-glove layer is workflow implementation, not just customer success.

We run an onboarding workshop to map the customer's pipeline into Mosaic workflows, align security and governance expectations, and set up integrations or API-driven automations. Then we close the loop with a post-onboarding success review anchored on measurable outcomes: time saved, throughput, and quality checks."

## Slide 10: TAM Mapping & CRM Automation Playbook
"We build TAM by combining enrichment and behavioral intent signals:
* enterprise and agency fit,
* and engagement with enterprise/automation/API surfaces.

We then tier accounts:
* Tier 1 goes to fast technical demo routing,
* Tier 2 into value content and guided onboarding,
* Tier 3 into long-term nurture.

CRM automation enforces SLAs so we do not miss high-intent moments."

## Slide 11: Customer Portfolio & 'Use-Case Empire'
"For Mosaic, the 'vertical empire' is not newsletter categories. It's workflow categories and segment motions.

B2C verticals are the template and workflow families: talking heads, podcasts, clips, montages, keynotes, webinars, and so on.

B2B motions map to team types: enterprise production pipelines, marketing content operations, and agencies delivering multi-client video assets. We align channel creative to these motions so we do not dilute the message."

## Slide 12: Sales & Marketing Symbiosis (Orchestrated Outbound)
"Sales and Marketing are a unified revenue pod.

Marketing provides a signal dossier: what they are interested in, which surfaces they engaged with, and the recommended pitch angle. Sales then runs the technical conversation with an integration and security-ready story.

We keep iteration fast with a bi-weekly pipeline council to adjust messaging pillars, routing rules, and channel allocation."

## Slide 13: AI Ops (GTM Automation Agents) - Net New
"This is the operational layer that makes our demand generation system scalable.

For a small company with CEO + Engineering and no dedicated sales team, we replace routing/scoring mechanics with demand-gen execution agents.

We run five AI Ops agents:
1) Growth Hacker Agent: prioritizes experiments (ICE) and designs viral/UGC loop plans.
2) Programmatic SEO Agent: produces page specs and drafts from templates + workflow outcomes.
3) Ad Creative Agent: generates fast ad copy/primary text variations and creative direction briefs for testing.
4) Social Content Agent: turns workflows into LinkedIn/Instagram carousel and reel scripts plus UGC prompt packs.
5) Marketing Intelligence Agent: cross-channel performance analysis across paid, content, and SEO using APIs/MCPs, feeding the next experiment queue updates.

The SLAs are weekly production batches, with fast creative iteration when early metrics change. Everything feeds back into experiment tracking so the team learns continuously."

## Slide 14: Risks & Mitigations
"We protect adoption trust across five risk families:
1) Security and compliance trust (SOC 2 + data posture may be questioned): mitigate with proactive documentation and enterprise-ready onboarding.
2) Integration friction (Pipeline mapping variance slows adoption): mitigate with standardized integration playbooks and white-glove help for edge cases.
3) Quality and adoption risk (Users doubt output reliability): mitigate via template-driven workflows and measurable success loops.
4) Enterprise sales latency (Sales cycle delays conversion): mitigate with intent routing and fast SLAs.
5) Messaging mismatch by persona (Creators vs admins vs ops see different messages): mitigate with persona-specific pillars and funnel content."

## Slide 15: 3-Month Roadmap (After Risks & Mitigations) - Net New
"Month 1 is foundation and instrumentation: align ICP and messaging, instrument intent surfaces, and ship AI Ops MVP for routing and scoring. We also produce the first template packs and security/integration collateral.

Month 2 is experiment execution: run B2C paid + content + community, and run B2B ABM + dark funnel routing plus security/integration content. Also initiate 2-4 creator or agency partnerships with measurable outcomes.

Month 3 is scaling and systematization: double down on what converts demos into technical onboarding. Expand template and integration coverage and scale AI Ops creative output into a weekly routine."

## Slide 16: Acquisition, Activation, Retention (AAR)
"This slide makes the growth system explicit.

Acquisition is not just traffic. It is qualified entry into the right motion:
* B2C through template discovery and creator distribution loops,
* B2B through enterprise and agency intent capture with ABM and technical content.

Activation is the speed to first real value:
* B2C: first usable output from a template/agent run,
* B2B: first successful team workflow run with governance/integration basics in place.

Retention is usage compounding:
* B2C: repeat runs and repurposing behavior,
* B2B: workspace growth, collaborator usage, trigger adoption, and API/workflow expansion.

This slide helps us avoid vanity metrics and focus on growth quality across the whole funnel."

## Slide 17: Unit Economics & CAC Payback
"We anchor value in Mosaic outcomes: time saved (40 hours/week on average), output volume (10x more videos), and scale evidence (500k+ videos processed).

B2C economics improve when PLG shortens time-to-value and reduces cost per activated creator. B2B economics improve when adoption expands across seats, shared workspaces, automations, and API usage.

The goal is not only to acquire customers; it's to create compounding usage that reduces CAC over time."

## Slide 18: Defining Success
"Success means repeatable activation and retention for creators, and consistent conversion from demos to onboarding for enterprise and agency customers.

Growth system success is measured by:
* improved CAC per activated creator,
* higher demo-to-onboarding conversion,
* and reduced time-to-routing for high-intent leads."
