## Slide 2: Executive Summary & The Mosaic Mandate
* Where we are today
  * Mosaic provides agentic AI video editing at scale: an interactive canvas, multimodal video understanding, and autopilot workflow execution.
  * Enterprise positioning emphasizes organization-wide access control, deep integrations, custom automations, and enterprise-grade security.
* The opportunity / mandate
  * Video editing is operationally expensive (time, coordination, consistency, and compliance).
  * Mosaic can convert those pain points into repeatable adoption motions by shipping "workflow outcomes" (time saved and output multipliers), not just editing features.
* The strategy (dual engine)
  * B2C engine: individual creators and small teams adopt Mosaic via templates, agents, and shareable workflows (fast activation, visible output, compounding community distribution).
  * B2B engine: enterprise teams and agencies adopt Mosaic by integrating into their production pipelines (MAM/storage + NLE roundtrip), automating workflows through event-driven triggers and APIs, and satisfying security/compliance requirements.
* The growth flywheel
  * B2C workflow visibility improves trust and proof (templates and agent outcomes).
  * B2B pipeline scales via intent signals on enterprise surfaces (/enterprise, API docs, integrations, automation triggers) and account routing that turns engagement into booked demos.

## Slide 4: The Exhaustion of Traditional Video Ops
* Why the old world breaks
  * Manual editing bottlenecks: repetitive tasks dominate and consistency suffers across editors and projects.
  * Fragmented toolchains: media asset management, NLEs, exports, and publishing are disconnected operationally.
  * Enterprise adoption friction: security reviews, access controls, auditability, and data retention requirements slow down team-wide rollouts.
* The Mosaic structural inversion
  * Build once in the canvas, then run on autopilot with event-driven triggers and automation.
  * Integrate into the pipeline (MAM/storage + NLE roundtrip exports like Premiere/Resolve/Final Cut).
  * Treat security and governance as first-class adoption requirements, not an afterthought.

## Slide 8: Referral Mechanisms & Professional Virality
* Why this must be different from consumer referral
  * Professionals and teams share only if the workflow is status-safe and genuinely useful.
* Mosaic referral mechanics (B2C and B2B)
  * B2C
    * Shareable templates and workflow outputs that are easy to show in a portfolio or team channel.
    * "Agent workflow unlocks" tied to verified engagement (reduce spam and bot farming).
    * **GitHub-like workflow community:** creators connect, collaborate, publish open workflows, and fork/remix each other's projects.
    * **UGC workflow video proof (LinkedIn + Instagram):** creators post short workflow runs and outcomes, with deep links back to the workflow/template repo so others fork/remix.
    * **Network effect:** every new creator increases workflow inventory and quality, which improves activation for the next cohort and attracts more creators.
  * B2B and agencies
    * Shared workspaces and permissions: invite editors and collaborators to reduce setup friction.
    * Agency-to-client workflow bundles: agencies can share internal templates to speed client onboarding.
* Fraud prevention (example approach)
  * Credit referrals only when the referred user triggers meaningful adoption signals (e.g., completed first edit/run and shared output or exported assets).

## Slide 9: White-Glove Workflow Implementation (Mosaic version)
* The problem

  * Implementation workshop: map the user's pipeline (assets, storage, NLE export needs, posting destinations) into Mosaic canvas workflows.
  * Security and governance readiness: RBAC model, audit log expectations, and data handling review aligned to SOC 2 and zero retention positioning.
  * Custom automations and API integration: event-driven triggers and webhooks for ingestion and delivery.
  * Post-onboarding success loop: iterate prompts/workflows using measured outcomes (time saved, output volume, and quality checks).

## Slide 14: Risks & Mitigations
* Security and compliance trust risk: SOC 2 + data posture may be questioned
  * Mitigation: proactive security documentation, SOC 2 Type II and zero retention messaging, and transparent data handling guardrails.
* Integration friction risk: Pipeline mapping variance slows adoption
  * Mitigation: standardized integration playbooks (MAM/storage + NLE roundtrip), plus white-glove onboarding for edge cases.
* Quality and adoption risk (agentic editing trust): Users doubt output reliability
  * Mitigation: quality review loop, template-driven workflows, and measurable time saved + output multipliers.
* Enterprise sales cycle latency: Sales cycle delays conversion
  * Mitigation: intent-triggered routing, technical demo readiness checklists, and fast follow-up SLAs.
* Messaging mismatch by persona (creator vs admin vs production ops): Creators vs admins vs ops see different messages
  * Mitigation: persona-specific messaging pillars and differentiated funnel content.
