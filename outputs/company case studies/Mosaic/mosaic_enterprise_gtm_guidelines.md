# Mosaic Enterprise GTM Strategy: Dual Engine Growth Playbook

This playbook describes how Mosaic can scale adoption across:
* B2C: individual creators and creator-led small teams
* B2B: enterprise video production teams and creative/production agencies

It follows the same "case-study playbook" intent as `tldr_gtm_guidelines.md` and `numeral_distribution_gtm_guidelines.md`, but specialized for Mosaic's agentic video editing workflow + enterprise pipeline integration requirements.

---

## 1. Market Dynamics & The Core Opportunity

### Why "video editing tools" are not enough anymore
Most teams are not trying to learn another editor. They are trying to:
* ship more content with consistent quality,
* reduce repetitive operational work,
* integrate video workflows into a real pipeline (asset management, NLE roundtrip, and publishing),
* and satisfy security/compliance expectations for organizational adoption.

### Mosaic's structural advantage (what we can sell repeatedly)
* Agentic workflow execution in a node-based canvas ("design once, run forever")
* Multimodal intelligence that understands video concepts/actions/spoken word/shot types
* Autopilot automation and event-driven triggers (batch processing, scheduled delivery)
* Enterprise governance and compliance positioning:
  * RBAC, shared workspaces, centralized asset library, audit logs
  * SOC 2 Type II positioning
  * zero data retention options and "no training on your data" posture
* Integration and programmability:
  * MAM/storage integration
  * NLE export/roundtrip via timeline XML (Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro)
  * API access, webhooks, and agent runs

## 2. Targeting Strategy: ICP and Buying Committees

## 2.1 B2C ICP: individual creators

### Who they are (practical definition)
* Creators repurposing multiple videos per week (podcasts, talking heads, webinars, clips)
* Editors or small teams who want reusable workflows rather than one-off edits
* Marketers producing repeatable content packages (intro/outro, captions, repurposing variations)

### What they value
* Fast time-to-first-output (activation)
* Repeatability across projects (workflow reuse)
* Shareable results that prove quality

### B2C audience chart (for the target audience slide)
Use this 3-column chart format: **Audience | Value | Want**

| Audience | Value | Want |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Creator-Entrepreneurs (YouTube / short-form operators) | Speed and output volume | Prompt-to-publish repurposing workflows that turn long-form into clips quickly |
| Creative Teams (in-house social/content pods) | Consistency and collaboration | Reusable template systems, shared assets, and fewer manual handoffs |
| Agency Creators / Freelance Editors | Throughput and client quality control | Repeatable multi-client workflows with fast iteration and predictable quality |

### Behavioral signals to track
* Template selection and first successful run
* Repeat runs using the same workflow/agent
* Content engine triggers usage (scheduled posting or batch processing)
* Sharing activity (public social posts or internal sharing in community/portfolio contexts)

## 2.2 B2B ICP: enterprise teams and agencies

### Enterprise buying committee
* Production/Media Ops owner: operational pipeline, throughput, and repeatability
* Editor lead: collaboration, permissions, and consistency
* Admin/Security/Compliance evaluator: RBAC, audit logs, security posture, data handling

### B2B buying committee table (for the target audience slide)
Use this 3-column format: **Buying committee | Target | Pain point**

| Buying committee | Target | Pain point |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Champion | Production/Media Ops Owner | Editing throughput bottlenecks, inconsistent handoffs, and missed delivery SLAs |
| Economic Buyer | Editors Lead / Head of Creative / Content Director | Rising production cost per asset and inability to scale quality with headcount |
| Technical Evaluator | IT / Security / Systems Admin | Tool sprawl risk, weak access controls, unclear auditability, and data-handling concerns |

### Agency buying committee (distinct motion)
* Agency operations and lead editors: multi-client consistency, scalable templating, and governance
* Account owner: speed-to-delivery and margin preservation through automation
* Client trust stakeholder: often overlaps with admin/security concerns

### Intent signals to route into B2B motion
* Engagement with `/enterprise` messaging about governance, integrations, and security
* Interest in API, automation, or event-driven triggers
* High dwell time on integrations/exports and security/compliance sections
* Demo bookings with technical/IT participation indicators

### Data execution flow (ABM foundation)
Keep this sequence explicit in audience strategy and slide narrative:
* **Technographic base**
* **Firmographic filter**

## 5. Channel Strategy: Paid, Content, Community, Partnerships (B2C + B2B)

The YC Growth/Marketing Intern posting explicitly emphasizes experiment-driven, multi-channel growth across `paid, content, community, partnerships`. This section operationalizes that by defining channel goals, watering holes, and experiment primitives for each segment.

### 5.1 B2C channel plan (individual creators)

## Paid
* Objective: drive template discovery and first successful runs
* Where ICP lives (examples to test):
  * creator and editing communities on major social platforms
  * interest clusters around podcasts/video editing and productivity
* Experiment primitives:
  * Template-specific landing pages (talking heads vs podcasts vs clip repurposing)
  * A/B creative packs that show before/after outcomes produced by the same workflow
  * "Prompt-to-outcome" variations: same template, different prompt scripts
* Success metrics:
  * Activation rate (time-to-first-output)
  * Cost per activated creator (validated run + share event)
  * Repeat-run rate within 7-14 days

## Content
* Objective: reduce friction to adoption by teaching repeatable workflows
* Where ICP lives:
  * search for "how to repurpose podcasts into clips", "podcast workflow automation", "talking head captions", etc.
* Experiment primitives:
  * Workflow outcome videos (short, proof-heavy)
  * Programmatic SEO pages mapped to template names and outcomes
  * Repurposing series: "one raw hour -> 20 clips" style outcome sequences
* Success metrics:
  * Organic-to-template conversion rate
  * Assisted activation rate from content pages
  * Returning creator behavior (repeat runs after content)

## Community
* Objective: create distribution that is native to creator behavior (sharing, iteration, feedback)
* Where ICP lives:
  * Discord/Slack-like creator communities (and public creator forums)
* Experiment primitives:
  * Weekly "edit challenge" using a template pack and share-to-unlock mechanic
  * Office hours: "workflow debugging" sessions
  * Creator spotlight drops: template pack breakdowns
  * GitHub-like workflow hub: open workflow repos, remix/fork mechanics, and contributor reputation layers
* Success metrics:
  * Participation rate and share-to-output rate
  * Community-sourced activated creators

### 5.3 Creator network effect system (GitHub-like community layer)
Build a community product layer where creators can connect, collaborate, and build on top of each other's workflows.

* Core experience:
  * Public workflow repositories with version history and changelogs
  * Fork/remix flow so creators can adapt workflows and publish improved variants
  * Collaboration layer: co-maintainers, comments, pull-request style suggestions, and issue threads for debugging
* Why it compounds:
  * More creators -> more workflows and higher-quality iterations
  * More workflows -> faster activation for new users (less blank-canvas friction)
  * Faster activation -> more creators contributing back into the network
* Flywheel:
  * New user adopts template/workflow -> forks and improves -> publishes improvement -> attracts more users -> expands reusable workflow graph
* Operating guardrails:
  * Featured maintainer program and quality scoring for trusted workflows
  * Moderation + anti-spam controls
  * Attribution and licensing controls for shared workflow assets

### UGC virality loop (LinkedIn + Instagram workflow videos)
Turn network value into repeatable public proof by encouraging creators to post workflow runs and outcomes as short videos on **LinkedIn** and **Instagram**.

* What to post (simple format)
  * Screen capture of the workflow run (or a quick “before → after”)
  * The final output (clip/video) plus a short caption on “what I changed”
  * A deep link back to the exact workflow/template repo (so others can fork/remix)
* Why it drives network growth
  * More UGC -> more workflow discovery -> more fork/remix attempts -> higher-quality workflows
  * Higher-quality workflows -> faster activation for new users -> more creators contributing
* How Mosaic enables it
  * One-click “Share workflow run” export designed for LinkedIn/Instagram
  * Auto-generated captions + suggested titles/hashtags
  * Version-linked attribution so improvements remain traceable back to the originating repo

## Partnerships
* Objective: trust transfer from creators to new creator adopters
* Where ICP lives:
  * YouTube-style and newsletter-like creator distribution
* Experiment primitives:
  * Creator partnerships with "template pack sponsorships"
  * Co-produced workflow tutorials with "run it on autopilot" hooks
* Success metrics:
  * Conversion rate from partner referral links
  * Template usage attributed to partner cohorts

## 9. Acquisition, Activation, Retention (AAR) Framework

This section defines the growth operating model by lifecycle stage, so channel execution and AI Ops automation can be judged on quality, not just volume.

## 9.1 Acquisition (bring in the right users)
* B2C acquisition
  * primary channels: template-led PLG, creator-focused paid campaigns, workflow outcome content, and creator partnerships
  * quality gate: acquisition is counted as meaningful only when users trigger activation events (not just visits/signups)
* B2B acquisition (enterprise + agencies)
  * primary channels: ABM outbound, dark funnel routing from enterprise/API/automation surfaces, role-targeted paid social, and partner channels
  * quality gate: prioritize persona-fit demos with technical stakeholder participation

## 9.2 Activation (time to first value)
* B2C activation definition
  * first usable video output from a template or prompt workflow
* B2B activation definition
  * first successful team workflow run with baseline governance/integration expectations met
* activation levers
  * template-first onboarding
  * implementation checklists by persona (creator, editor, production ops, admin/security)
  * fast technical handoffs (routing/scoring SLAs)

## 9.3 Retention (compounding usage)
* B2C retention
  * repeat-run behavior, repurposing frequency, and workflow reuse across projects
* B2B retention
  * shared workspace depth, collaborator growth, trigger/automation usage, and API expansion
* retention interventions
  * recurring workflow optimization reviews
  * persona-specific enablement and nurture
  * quality assurance loops for output confidence

## 9.4 AAR operating cadence
* weekly:
  * review acquisition source quality and activation lag
  * rotate creative variants from AI Ops creative generator
* bi-weekly:
  * evaluate activation bottlenecks by persona and channel
* monthly:
  * review retention cohorts, expansion signals, and lifecycle conversion leakage

## 11. Risks & Mitigations
* Security and compliance trust risk
  * Mitigation: proactive security documentation and enterprise onboarding checklists aligned to SOC 2, zero retention, and no training posture.
* Integration friction risk
  * Mitigation: standardized integration playbooks + white-glove implementation for edge cases.
* Adoption and quality risk
  * Mitigation: template-driven workflows, quality review loops, and measurable outcome validation (time saved, output volume).
* Enterprise sales cycle latency
  * Mitigation: intent-triggered routing, fast follow-up SLAs, and technical demo readiness gates.
* Messaging mismatch risk by persona
  * Mitigation: differentiated messaging pillars for creators, production ops, and security evaluators.
