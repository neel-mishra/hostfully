## Executive Summary

Gmail's Promotions tab is a classification system, not a penalty. Newsletters trigger commercial signals — bulk sending, templates, tracking pixels, unsubscribe headers — that Gmail uses to sort email into Promotions. This is by design and affects virtually all newsletters regardless of quality.

The critical insight for newsletter operators: Gmail's tab classification is per-user, not per-sender. Individual subscriber behavior — open frequency, replies, manual tab moves, contact list additions — determines where each email appears for each recipient. This means sender-side "fixes" have limited impact.

Practical levers that work: sending from a named individual rather than a brand, encouraging replies during onboarding, asking subscribers to move emails to Primary, and aggressively cleaning inactive subscribers.

Promotions placement is not inherently problematic. TLDR operates at 7M+ subscribers with 40-48% open rates from the Promotions tab. Engagement quality — not tab placement — drives newsletter performance. Most deliverability complaints are engagement problems misdiagnosed as infrastructure issues.
