## Executive Summary

Milk Road, a daily crypto newsletter founded by Shaan Puri and Ben Levy, grew from zero subscribers to a reported seven-figure exit in 10 months during 2022. The newsletter addressed a specific gap: crypto professionals lacked a single, curated daily briefing and were instead scattered across Discord, Twitter, and Telegram.

Three factors drove the rapid trajectory. First, Shaan Puri brought an existing audience, giving the newsletter a distribution tailwind from day one. Second, the team sequenced priorities — subscriber acquisition first, monetization second, data and self-serve tools third — rather than pursuing all three simultaneously. Third, the editorial team wrote all sponsored content in-house, making ads indistinguishable from the newsletter's editorial voice. This native approach reduced banner blindness and increased advertiser ROI.

The two-sided network model was central. Readers attracted advertisers. Advertiser revenue funded subscriber growth. The flywheel accelerated with each cycle. Milk Road reached 250,000+ subscribers and attracted premium sponsors before selling to Stacked Marketer.

TLDR operates a structurally similar model across 12 newsletters reaching 7M+ tech professionals. The flagship newsletter maintains 48% open rates — roughly 3x the industry average. Advertisers like Redact have seen 50% lower CPC than LinkedIn through TLDR placements, precisely because native ads in a high-trust newsletter environment outperform programmatic channels.

The key implication for newsletter operators: sequencing beats multitasking. Build the audience first, monetize once the base is large enough to command premium rates, and treat ads as part of the editorial product rather than an interruption to it. Milk Road's speed was partly circumstantial — crypto bull market timing and founder distribution aren't replicable — but the operational playbook is.
