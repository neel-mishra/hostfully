# Video Script: How To Build a Fanbase — From Followers to True Fans

## Hook (0-5s)
- **Visual**: Counter showing "50,000 followers" flickering, then a smaller counter showing "$200K revenue" from "2,000 fans." Bold text: "Followers ≠ Fans."
- **Audio**: "50,000 followers are worth less than 2,000 true fans. Here's proof."

## Body (Main Content)

### Scene 1: The Extra Points Story
- **Visual**: Newsletter screenshot with "Extra Points" header. Stats animated in: "27,000 readers → 2,000 paying → $200K/year." Calculator showing $100/fan.
- **Audio**: "Matt Brown runs Extra Points, a college sports business newsletter. Twenty-seven thousand total readers. Only two thousand pay. Those two thousand generate over 200K a year."

### Scene 2: Audiences vs. Fans
- **Visual**: Two columns appearing. Left: "Audience" with behaviors (reads, scrolls, forgets). Right: "Fanbase" with behaviors (buys, shares, recruits). Right side glows.
- **Audio**: "Audiences consume your content. Fans advocate for it. They buy what you sell, share what you write, and recruit their friends without being asked."

### Scene 3: The Platform Trap
- **Visual**: Social media logos (Instagram, TikTok, Twitter) on quicksand, sinking. An email icon floating above on solid ground.
- **Audio**: "Building on social platforms is building on rented land. EssentiallySports learned this — they converted their social audience into 1.5 million email subscribers with 45 percent open rates. Zero paid acquisition."

### Scene 4: The Playbook
- **Visual**: Four cards flipping over, each revealing a strategy: "Own the relationship" → "Collaborate to borrow trust" → "Reward first fans" → "Stay consistent, then evolve."
- **Audio**: "Four moves that build real fans: own the relationship through email, collaborate with trusted peers, reward your earliest supporters, and stay consistent — then evolve based on what your audience wants."

## Call to Action
- **Visual**: Text overlay: "Followers are vanity. Fans are revenue." TLDR blog link.
- **Audio**: "Followers are vanity. Fans are revenue. Full case studies and strategies on the TLDR blog."
