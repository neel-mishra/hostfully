# Why Your Emails Are Going to Gmail's Promotions Tab

Gmail sorts about 1.8 billion accounts' worth of email every day. If your newsletter keeps landing in Promotions, that's not a bug — it's Gmail working exactly as designed.

We run 12 newsletters at TLDR with open rates between 40-48%. Our emails land in Promotions for plenty of subscribers. It hasn't stopped us from building a 7M+ subscriber base. Here's what's actually going on, and what you can do about it.

## Promotions Is Not Spam

This is the most important distinction in email deliverability.

Spam means Gmail blocked your message. It's hidden, never seen, and auto-deleted in 30 days. Promotions means Gmail delivered your message and filed it in a specific tab. The email is searchable, visible, and waiting for the reader.

Promotions is a filing cabinet. Spam is the trash.

## Why Gmail Puts You There

Gmail's algorithm looks at patterns, not intent. It doesn't care whether your email is valuable — it cares whether your email *looks like* marketing. The signals that trigger Promotions classification:

- **Bulk sending.** You're emailing thousands of people at once.
- **Templated design.** Same layout, same structure, every send.
- **Scheduled cadence.** Daily, weekly, or monthly — predictable timing signals a campaign.
- **Promotional language.** "Sale," "discount," "limited time," "free" — Gmail reads these the way you'd expect.
- **Image-heavy design.** More images than text signals marketing.
- **Low engagement.** If recipients don't open, click, or reply, Gmail takes the hint.

Most newsletters hit at least four of these. That's why newsletters land in Promotions by default — not because they're bad, but because they structurally look like marketing emails.

## Engagement Moves You to Primary

Gmail personalizes tab placement per recipient. The same email from the same sender can land in Primary for one person and Promotions for another. The difference is engagement history.

Signals that tell Gmail to move you to Primary:

- The recipient opens your emails consistently
- They reply (even once matters)
- They drag your email from Promotions to Primary
- They add you to their contacts
- They click links in your emails

This is why open rates compound. Engaged readers train Gmail to prioritize your messages, which drives more visibility, which drives more engagement. The opposite is also true — disengaged subscribers actively hurt your placement.

## When Promotions Placement Actually Hurts

Promotions is fine if your audience checks that tab. Many people browse it regularly for deals and updates.

It becomes a problem in three scenarios:

1. **Your audience never opens the Promotions tab.** Your email is delivered but never seen.
2. **Your open rates are dropping.** Promotions placement accelerates the decline — lower visibility means fewer opens, which means Gmail deprioritizes you further.
3. **Your subscribers don't know where to find you.** They think you stopped emailing. You're just buried.

## What You Can Actually Do

You can't force Gmail to put you in Primary. But you can improve your odds.

**Ask subscribers to move you.** In your welcome email and occasionally in your newsletter, tell readers: "Drag this email to your Primary tab so you don't miss future issues." Gmail will ask if they want to apply the change to all future messages from you. It works.

**Ask them to add you to contacts.** One of the strongest signals Gmail respects.

**Encourage replies.** A reply tells Gmail this is a conversation, not a broadcast. TLDR includes reply prompts in onboarding emails for exactly this reason.

**Send consistently.** Erratic sending patterns confuse Gmail's classification. Pick a schedule and stick to it. TLDR has published every weekday for years — consistency is a deliverability signal, not just an editorial one.

**Watch your engagement metrics.** A sudden drop in opens or clicks can trigger reclassification. If you notice a shift, audit your content and list hygiene before blaming Gmail.

## Why Emails Suddenly Move to Promotions

If your emails used to land in Primary and stopped, something changed. Common triggers:

- You increased sending volume or frequency
- You changed your email template or added more images
- Your engagement metrics dropped
- Gmail updated its algorithm (this happens regularly)
- A chunk of your subscribers stopped opening

These shifts aren't always reversible at scale. The fix is almost always the same: give people a reason to open, and Gmail will follow.

## The Real Answer

Gmail's Promotions tab isn't your enemy. It's a classification system doing its job.

The emails that escape Promotions aren't using a secret trick. They're earning engagement — consistent opens, replies, and trust built over time. At TLDR, we focus on sending emails worth opening every single day. That's the strategy. Everything else is a tactic in service of that one idea.
