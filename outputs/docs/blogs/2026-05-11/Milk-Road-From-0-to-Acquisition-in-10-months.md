# Milk Road: From 0 to Acquisition in 10 Months

Milk Road went from zero subscribers to a reported seven-figure exit in 10 months. Not 10 years. Months.

Shaan Puri and Ben Levy launched a daily crypto newsletter in early 2022, grew it past 250K subscribers, and sold it to Stacked Marketer before the year was out. The playbook wasn't complicated — but most newsletter operators skip at least half of it.

## They Found a Gap and Filled It Fast

Crypto in 2022 meant drowning in Discord servers, Twitter threads, and Telegram channels. Nobody had time for all of it. Most people had time for none of it.

Milk Road fixed that with a daily email. Five minutes. Plain English. No jargon, no shilling.

That's the same insight behind TLDR — busy professionals want signal, not noise. TLDR's flagship newsletter hits 48% open rates doing exactly this for tech. Milk Road proved the model works in crypto too.

## The Two-Sided Model

Newsletters are two-sided networks. Readers show up for content. Advertisers show up for readers. Milk Road nailed both sides early.

**Readers:** The audience was crypto-curious professionals — not just degens. People who wanted to know what was happening without living on Crypto Twitter. Read this in five minutes, skip everything else.

**Advertisers:** Web3 companies were spending aggressively in 2022. Milk Road gave them a concentrated, engaged audience with native ads written in the newsletter's voice. Readers actually read them, so CPCs stayed low and performance stayed high.

TLDR sees the same dynamic across its 12 newsletters. Redact saw 50% lower CPC than LinkedIn through TLDR ads because native placements in a trusted newsletter don't get the banner-blindness treatment. Milk Road understood this from day one.

## The Flywheel That Drove the Exit

More subscribers meant more advertiser demand. More revenue meant more budget for growth. More growth meant more subscribers. Simple concept. Hard to execute fast.

Milk Road pulled it off in months for three reasons:

1. **Shaan Puri had distribution.** His Twitter following and My First Million podcast gave Milk Road a launch pad most newsletters never get. That's a tailwind, not a repeatable tactic.
2. **Referral programs worked.** Incentivizing subscribers to share created organic growth on top of paid acquisition.
3. **Timing.** Early-to-mid 2022 still had bull market energy. Demand for crypto content was high. By the time the market turned, Milk Road already had scale.

Being honest about these tailwinds matters. The strategy was smart, but the conditions were favorable too.

## Three Growth Phases — Executed in Sequence

**Phase 1: Subscriber acquisition.** Paid ads, referrals, SEO, and Shaan's existing audience. Every dollar went toward growing the list. Revenue was secondary.

**Phase 2: Monetization.** Once the base was large enough to attract sponsors, Milk Road launched additional crypto-focused newsletters to segment the audience. Advertisers could target specific niches. Milk Road had more inventory to sell.

**Phase 3: Data and self-serve.** Deeper audience analytics, performance-based pricing, and self-serve ad tools made the business scalable — and acquirable.

Most newsletter operators try all three simultaneously. Milk Road did them in order. That discipline is underrated.

## Content and Ads Were the Same Team

Milk Road's editorial team wrote the ads. Most people gloss over this detail, but it's the reason the ads worked.

When the same writers who produce your editorial also write your ad copy, the ads don't feel like ads. They match the voice. Readers don't skip them. Advertisers get better results.

TLDR does the same thing — copywriting services included at no extra cost. Campaign reporting included too. Advertisers know exactly what they're getting. No black box.

It's one of the highest-leverage decisions a newsletter business can make.

## What Newsletter Operators Should Take From This

Milk Road's 10-month run isn't a template you can copy. Shaan's audience, the crypto bull market, and the timing were unique. But the principles transfer.

- **Solve one problem clearly.** Milk Road saved people time on crypto news. No feature creep, no pivot, no "platform."
- **Build both sides of the network.** Readers without advertisers don't generate revenue. Advertisers without readers don't stick around.
- **Sequence your priorities.** Subscribers first, monetization second, optimization third. In that order.
- **Make ads part of the product.** Write them in-house. Match the voice. Include reporting. Advertisers stay longer when they see results.
- **Know your tailwinds.** Milk Road had founder distribution and market timing. Be honest about what's replicable and what isn't.

250K subscribers and a seven-figure exit in under a year is impressive. But the lesson isn't "do what Milk Road did." It's do these five things in sequence, find your own tailwind, and don't stop.
