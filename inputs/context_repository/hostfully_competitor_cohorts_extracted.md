## Sheet: Strategy Notes
-  | Two-Cohort Strategy: How to Use This With Our Google Rep
-  | The Problem With a Single Competitor List
-  | If our competitor cohort is biased toward players like Guesty, the only opportunities we see are the ones they’re already dominating. It becomes a money question — can we outspend them? (We can’t.)
-  | What Cohort 1 Tells Us (Above Us)
-  | •  What the spending ceiling looks like on high-value keywords
-  | •  Which keywords are money fights we should avoid
-  | •  Where the big players are bidding on our brand name (brand defense)
-  | •  Market-level trends in messaging and positioning
-  | What Cohort 2 Tells Us (Around & Below Us)
-  | •  Keywords where smaller competitors are winning that we could outbid affordably
-  | •  Brand name conquesting opportunities (bid on their names cheaply)
-  | •  Strategy discovery — what are they doing that’s working at lower budgets?
-  | •  Keyword gaps where nobody is competing — these are our cheapest wins
-  | •  Hostify brand confusion: people may search “Hostify” and mean us, or vice versa
-  | What We’re Asking Our Rep For
-  | •  Auction insights split by cohort, not blended
-  | •  Impression share data: where are we losing to Cohort 2 that we could win?
-  | •  Keyword overlap analysis: which terms do Cohort 2 competitors bid on that we don’t?
-  | •  Brand name bidding: which competitors (both cohorts) are bidding on “Hostfully”?
-  | •  Cost comparison: what’s the avg CPC fighting Cohort 1 vs Cohort 2?
