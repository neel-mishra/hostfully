# Hostfully Paid Ads Design Bible

## Brand-Led Performance Design Thesis
The design system balances three goals:
1. **Immediate platform stop power**
2. **Rapid comprehension in under 2 seconds**
3. **Trustworthy SaaS brand continuity across high-volume variants**

## 1. Logo System
- Preferred position: top-left.
- Minimum inset: `6-10%` from edges depending on format.
- Use white logo on dark/gradient backgrounds; blue logo on light backgrounds.
- Maintain clear space equal to at least the logo mark height.
- Avoid placing nearby competing iconography in logo zone.

## 2. Typography Architecture

## Primary Type Behavior
- Use short, high-contrast headline blocks.
- Combine regular and bold to emphasize one conversion token.
- Favor sentence case for main headline; all-caps for sublabel/campaign line.

## Message Rhythm
- Most effective rhythm: `problem/question` + `promised outcome`.
- Keep line breaks intentional and chunked by meaning, not by arbitrary width.

## Recommended Type Tokens
- H1 (mobile-heavy): `52-84 px` depending on format and crop density.
- H2/support: `28-44 px`.
- Eyebrow label: `20-30 px` all caps, high tracking discipline.
- CTA label: `38-54 px` in bold for thumb-stop readability.

## 3. Color System

## Core Palette Roles
- **Brand blue/violet:** authority and consistency.
- **Mint/teal:** momentum, growth, supportive tone.
- **Yellow CTA:** action and contrast.
- **White text:** legibility on dark/complex surfaces.
- **Coral accents:** highlights for channel badges and contrast moments.

## Background Strategies
- **Flat fills:** cleaner, bolder, direct value communication.
- **Soft gradients:** premium and modern, especially for feature-rich ads.
- **Photo + color overlay:** proof/testimonial storytelling while preserving readability.

## Contrast Rule
Ensure each ad has at least one high-contrast triad:
- background field
- primary headline
- CTA button

If one weakens, strengthen the others.

## 4. CTA System

## CTA Visual Anatomy
- Shape: full pill or rounded rectangle with large corner radius.
- Fill: yellow default for primary conversion actions.
- Label: concise, plain language.
- Shadow: subtle depth, never heavy/neon.

## CTA Language Ladder
- Low friction: `Let us help`
- Story proof: `Read the Story`, `Read Yossi's Story`
- Action blueprint: `View the Blueprint`

## CTA Placement
- Never obscure main proof object.
- Keep in lower-left/lower-mid safe zone.
- Preserve breathing room from frame edges and subject cutouts.

## 5. Composition and Spacing System

## Spatial Hierarchy
- Top-left: identity
- Mid-left: message
- Right/center: product/proof
- Lower-left: conversion

## Spacing Discipline
- Respect vertical rhythm between logo, headline, and CTA.
- Keep text block edges aligned for a clean professional structure.
- Avoid overfilling: whitespace is used as confidence signal.

## 6. Product Depiction Rules
- UI should read as plausible product reality, not abstract decoration.
- Keep key UI details crisp where they support trust (calendar bars, cards, toggles, labels).
- Use interface crop to direct attention to value, not full product complexity.
- Add floating UI/support icons only when they reinforce capability.

## 7. Emotional Layering Strategy
- **Rational conversion layer:** metrics, workflow, before/after proof.
- **Emotional layer:** relief, confidence, support, aspiration.
- The strongest creatives harmonize both, not one in isolation.

## 8. Varianting Framework
- Keep constants fixed:
  - logo placement
  - CTA structure
  - layout scaffold
  - brand palette
- Rotate variables:
  - hook copy
  - emphasized token
  - hero crop
  - accent hue
  - proof mechanism

This creates volume without brand drift.

## 9. Quality Control Checklist
- Is the hook readable at arm's length on mobile?
- Is one keyword or numeric token clearly dominant?
- Is the CTA instantly visible and understandable?
- Is brand identity visible within first glance?
- Is there visual proof supporting the claim?
- Are edge margins and safe zones respected for the target format?

## 10. Common Failure Modes to Avoid
- Overcrowded text blocks with no dominant token.
- Weak contrast between headline and background.
- CTA blending into the palette instead of standing out.
- Novel layouts that break logo/headline/CTA pattern memory.
- Decorative elements that compete with value proposition.
