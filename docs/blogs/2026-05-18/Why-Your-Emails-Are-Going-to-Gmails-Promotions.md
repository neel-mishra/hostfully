# Why Your Emails Land in Gmail's Promotions Tab (And Why That's Fine)

Gmail's Promotions tab isn't spam. Your emails are delivered. They're just sorted into a different folder — one that over a billion Gmail users browse on purpose.

Most senders panic when they see their open rates dip and assume Promotions placement is a penalty. It's not. Gmail separates commercial email from personal email because users asked for it. The tab is organizational, not punitive.

TLDR sends to millions of Gmail inboxes every weekday. Our flagship newsletter holds a 48% open rate — more than double the industry average of 15-20%. Promotions tab placement hasn't stopped that. Here's what actually matters.

## How Gmail Sorts Your Email

Gmail looks at a handful of signals to decide where an email goes:

- **Content patterns.** Promotional language, discount codes, and marketing keywords push emails toward Promotions. Write like a person, not a billboard.
- **Sender reputation.** Consistent engagement, low spam complaints, and proper authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) all help. Reputation is earned over months, not configured in an afternoon.
- **Recipient behavior.** If a user opens, reads, and replies to your emails, Gmail notices. Individual engagement is the strongest placement signal — stronger than anything you can do on the sending side.
- **Email structure.** Heavy HTML templates with big images, multiple CTAs, and minimal text look like marketing to Gmail's classifier. Because they are.

The weighting is personalized. The same email from the same sender can land in Primary for one user and Promotions for another, depending on each person's history with that sender.

## Why Newsletters Almost Always Hit Promotions

Newsletters check every box Gmail uses to identify commercial email: bulk sending, scheduled delivery, consistent templates, broad audiences. That's not a flaw in Gmail's system — it's the system working as designed.

TLDR's newsletters land in Promotions for many subscribers. We still hit 48% open rates across 7M+ subscribers. The tab isn't the problem. Whether subscribers trust you enough to open — that's the problem.

## What You Can Actually Do About It

### Get subscribers to tell Gmail they want your email

User actions are the most powerful signal. Nothing you do on the sending side outweighs a subscriber dragging your email from Promotions to Primary. Gmail asks them to confirm, and after that, future emails land in Primary for that user.

Tell your subscribers to:

- **Drag your email to Primary.** Gmail will ask if they want to keep doing this. One action, permanent fix for that user.
- **Add your sending address to Contacts.** Gmail treats contact-list senders as personal connections.
- **Reply to an email.** Even once. Replies create a conversational signal that's hard for Gmail's classifier to ignore.

Put these instructions in your welcome email. Most subscribers will never do it unprompted.

### Send emails that don't look like marketing

- Use minimal HTML. Plain-text-style emails with a clean layout outperform heavy templates in inbox placement and engagement.
- Limit links. Every additional link is a signal to Gmail's classifier.
- Skip the salesy language. "EXCLUSIVE DEAL" and "Don't miss out!!!" are classifier bait.
- Include a plain text version. Some filters weight it favorably, and it's good practice regardless.
- Keep a consistent sending schedule. Sporadic volume looks suspicious.

None of this guarantees Primary placement. But it tilts the odds, and it also makes your emails better.

## Promotions Is Not Spam

This distinction matters more than any placement strategy.

**Spam** means Gmail thinks your email is unwanted or harmful. Users never see it. Your sender reputation takes real damage. Recovery is slow and painful.

**Promotions** means Gmail thinks your email is commercial. Users see it, search for it, browse it. Your sender reputation is intact. You're delivered — just categorized.

If your emails are hitting Spam, you have a deliverability crisis. If they're hitting Promotions, you have a sorting preference. These are different problems by orders of magnitude.

## When Promotions Placement Actually Hurts

Promotions becomes a real problem when:

- Open rates drop because subscribers don't check the tab
- Subscribers don't know their emails are being sorted there
- Your content isn't compelling enough to seek out

The fix for all three is the same: build trust, deliver value, and tell subscribers where to find you. TLDR has published every weekday for years. Consistency is the actual deliverability strategy — not a hack, not a workaround.

## The Short Version

Gmail's Promotions tab is inbox organization, not a penalty. Your emails are delivered. Most newsletters will land there for most Gmail users, and that's fine.

Focus on what moves the needle: send emails worth opening, maintain a clean sender reputation, and make it easy for subscribers to tell Gmail they want your email in Primary. Everything else is optimization at the margins.
