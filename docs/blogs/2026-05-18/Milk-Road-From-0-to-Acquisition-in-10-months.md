# How Milk Road Hit 250K Subscribers and Sold for $5M+ in 10 Months

Shaan Puri and Ben Levy launched Milk Road in early 2022. A free daily crypto newsletter. Five-minute read. Ten months later, they sold it for a reported $5M+.

Zero subscribers to acquisition in under a year. Here's how that actually worked.

## The Format Was the Strategy

Crypto in 2022 was chaos. Token launches, rug pulls, regulatory drama, new L2s every week. Most crypto media either assumed you had a finance degree or read like a Discord shitpost.

Milk Road picked a lane: explain today's crypto news in five minutes, in plain English, with personality. That's it. No deep-dive research reports. No 30-minute podcasts. One email, every morning, written like a smart friend catching you up.

The format wasn't new — TLDR has run the same playbook in tech for years. But Milk Road executed it in a niche that was starving for clarity.

## beehiiv Did the Heavy Lifting

Milk Road ran on beehiiv, which handled publishing, subscriber management, and analytics out of the box. No custom tech stack. No engineering hires.

This matters more than it sounds. Most newsletter operators spend their first six months building infrastructure. Milk Road spent it acquiring subscribers.

beehiiv's built-in recommendation engine also gave them free cross-promotion with other newsletters on the platform. Every new beehiiv newsletter in the crypto space became a potential distribution partner.

## The Referral Program Drove Viral Growth

Milk Road's referral program was dead simple: refer friends, get Milk Road merch. T-shirts, hats, stickers. Readers became recruiters.

The genius was the branding. Crypto readers love signaling tribe membership. Wearing Milk Road merch wasn't just a freebie — it was identity. And every piece of merch was a walking ad.

beehiiv automated the whole system. Tracking, reward tiers, fulfillment triggers. The team didn't have to manage referrals manually. They set it up, and it ran.

This created a growth flywheel: good content drove opens, opens drove referrals, referrals drove subscriber count, subscriber count attracted advertisers, ad revenue funded more growth.

## 100% Ad-Supported, Zero Paywall

Milk Road never charged readers a cent. Revenue was 100% advertising.

The model worked because the audience was specific and valuable. Crypto enthusiasts with disposable income, high engagement, and purchase intent. Advertisers — exchanges, DeFi protocols, wallet providers — paid a premium to reach them.

Three ad tiers per issue:

- **Top placement:** Maximum visibility, premium pricing, full branding
- **Mid-placement:** Solid exposure at a lower price point
- **Quick links at the bottom:** Cheap entry point for smaller advertisers

Milk Road wrote the ad copy in-house, matching their editorial voice. This kept the reading experience consistent and boosted click-through rates. Advertisers got performance reports included.

At TLDR, we run a similar model: 3 ad slots per newsletter, copy written by our team, performance data included. The approach works because readers don't feel like they're being sold to. They feel like they're reading a curated briefing that happens to include relevant sponsors.

## The Numbers That Mattered

Milk Road tracked what actually predicted growth:

**Reader side:** Total subscribers. Daily open rate. Whether readers felt informed in under five minutes.

**Advertiser side:** Revenue per issue. How many advertisers renewed. Whether sponsors saw measurable ROI.

Open rates reportedly hit 40-48% — roughly 2-3x the industry average of 15-20%. That's the number that made advertisers line up.

For context, TLDR's flagship newsletter hits 48% open rates across 7M+ subscribers. High open rates aren't magic. They're the result of showing up every day with content readers actually want.

## Why It Sold So Fast

250,000 engaged subscribers in a high-value niche. 40%+ open rates. A clean ad-supported revenue model with repeatable demand. A team of two running the whole operation on beehiiv.

That's an acquisition target.

The reported $5M+ price was roughly $20 per subscriber — high, but defensible for a crypto audience with proven engagement and advertiser demand. The acquirer got a distribution channel, an audience, and a brand, all in one deal.

## What Newsletter Operators Should Take From This

Milk Road didn't invent anything. Daily email, niche audience, referral program, ad model. Every piece existed before them.

What they did was execute fast, stay focused, and pick a niche with real demand and no clean incumbent. Five decisions mattered:

1. **Niche with urgency.** Crypto moves daily. Readers needed a daily newsletter. The format fit the market.
2. **Platform over custom build.** beehiiv let them launch in days, not months. Speed mattered more than customization.
3. **Merch as referral incentive.** Tangible, brandable, shareable. Better than discounts on a free product.
4. **Ad-only revenue.** No paywall friction meant faster subscriber growth. Monetize attention, not access.
5. **Exit when the numbers peak.** They sold at 250K subscribers in a crypto bull market. Timing was part of the strategy.

The playbook isn't complicated. The execution is.
