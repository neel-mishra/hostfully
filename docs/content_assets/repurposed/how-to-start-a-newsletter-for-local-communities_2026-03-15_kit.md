A friend asked me: "How do I start a newsletter for my neighborhood?"

My answer: stop thinking like a media company. Start thinking like a neighbor who pays attention.

Cover what people actually need to know:
- What did the city council decide?
- Which restaurants just opened?
- What's happening this weekend?

Skip the rest. Your readers have 5 minutes, not 50.

The monetization follows naturally. Local businesses want to reach exactly the audience you've built. A coffee shop, a real estate agent, a community event — they'll sponsor you before you hit 1,000 subscribers.

Start with trust. Add great content. Don't stop.

## Executive Summary

The decline of local newspapers and the noise of social media feeds have created an opportunity for local newsletters — hyper-focused publications serving specific communities with curated, timely information.

The key insight is that local newsletters succeed through specificity, not breadth. Defining a tight audience ("parents in one school district" rather than "everyone in the city") drives every downstream decision: content selection, growth tactics, and monetization.

Content strategy focuses on four pillars: need-to-know decisions, context on confusing local issues, weekend picks, and practical resources. The editorial principle is curation over comprehensiveness — if content doesn't help someone plan their week or understand their neighborhood, it gets cut.

Growth channels differ from national newsletters. Word of mouth drives the most sustainable acquisition. Local business partnerships, evergreen SEO content, and offline tactics (QR codes, community events) outperform paid social for community-focused publications.

Monetization is viable at small scale. Local sponsorships, premium subscriptions, and affiliate partnerships work with as few as 2,000 engaged subscribers because the audience is hyper-targeted and high-trust. The critical principle: plan monetization from day one, not as an afterthought.

Common pitfalls include over-scoping content, depending on social platforms for distribution, and delaying reader engagement. The most successful local newsletters treat consistency as their primary competitive advantage.
