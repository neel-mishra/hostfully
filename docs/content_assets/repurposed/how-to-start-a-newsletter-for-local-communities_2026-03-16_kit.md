Local news is dying. Newsrooms have cut staff by 60% since 2005. Most neighborhoods now have zero dedicated reporters.

But newsletters are filling that gap — and they have one massive advantage over national publications: the content is inherently scarce. Nobody else is covering your city council meeting or your neighborhood restaurant opening.

The playbook for starting one:

→ Pick a community, not a city. "Downtown Austin parents" is a newsletter. "Austin news" is a newspaper.
→ Cover 5 things, not 50. Curation is the job.
→ Send weekly. Consistency beats frequency every time.
→ Grow through the community — word of mouth, local partnerships, QR codes at farmers markets.

You don't need 100K subscribers to monetize. 2,000 engaged local readers + one sponsor per issue = a real business.

## Executive Summary

The local news crisis has created an opening for newsletter-based community publishing. With newsroom staffing down 60% since 2005 and social media algorithms controlling visibility, email newsletters offer community-focused publishers a direct, owned distribution channel.

The most effective local newsletters share four characteristics: hyper-specific audience definition (a neighborhood or community segment, not an entire city), curated rather than comprehensive coverage (five essential items per issue, not fifty), consistent weekly cadence that builds reader habits, and community-embedded growth strategies.

Growth channels for local newsletters differ fundamentally from national publications. Word-of-mouth referrals from genuinely useful content drive the most subscribers. Partnerships with local businesses and organizations provide cross-promotion. Evergreen search content ("Best Parks in [City]") generates steady signup flow. Physical distribution tactics — QR codes at community events, flyers at coffee shops — leverage geographic proximity that national newsletters can't.

Monetization requires far fewer subscribers than national publications. Local newsletters can generate meaningful revenue with 2,000-3,000 engaged subscribers through local business sponsorships ($50-200/week per sponsor), community membership tiers ($5-10/month), and affiliate partnerships with local commerce platforms. The key constraint is planning for monetization from launch rather than treating it as an afterthought.

Common failure modes include attempting comprehensive coverage instead of curation, building distribution exclusively on social media platforms, delaying direct subscriber engagement, and deferring monetization planning.
