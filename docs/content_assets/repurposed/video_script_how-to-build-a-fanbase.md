# Video Script: From Followers to True Fans

**Source:** How-To-Build-a-Fanbase-From-Followers-to-True-Fans.md
**Duration:** 60 seconds
**Format:** Vertical (TikTok/Reels)

## Hook (0-5s)
- **Visual**: Big number on screen: "48%" with a smaller strikethrough "15–20%" and label "INDUSTRY AVG". Quick cut to "NOT LIST SIZE — RELATIONSHIP"
- **Audio**: "Forty-eight percent opens — more than double typical newsletters."

## Body (5-45s)

### Scene 1 (5-15s)
- **Visual**: Three labels stack: SUBSCRIBERS / CUSTOMERS / FANS — "FANS" glows. Text: "FANS BUY, SHARE, RECRUIT"
- **Narration**: Kevin Kelly was right: you do not need millions. Subscribers get the content, customers pay, fans do both and pull other people in. A fan forwards your email; a follower scrolls past.

### Scene 2 (15-25s)
- **Visual**: Phone with algorithm maze vs simple "INBOX" graphic. Caption: "OWN THE RELATIONSHIP"
- **Narration**: One platform can rug you tomorrow. Email is the channel where nobody else's algorithm sits between you and the reader. Build there first.

### Scene 3 (25-35s)
- **Visual**: Social clip teasing a headline, arrow to "FULL STORY IN EMAIL". B-roll: rough notes, not perfect polish.
- **Narration**: Use social for discovery, not as home. Tease the best bit, then send people to the newsletter or site. Show the messy process — polished feeds rarely make fans.

### Scene 4 (35-45s)
- **Visual**: Calendar with weekdays checked, text "SHOW UP · REPLY · SEGMENT"
- **Narration**: Ask for replies and actually read them. Segment by what people click. Consistency beats one viral hit — show up, deliver, repeat.

## Call to Action (45-60s)
- **Visual**: Text overlay: "HOW TO BUILD A FANBASE — LINK IN BIO" plus handle
- **Audio**: "Full playbook on turning followers into fans — link in bio."

## Notes
- **Tone**: Practical creator advice, confident but not hustle-bro — sounds like someone who reads the data.
- **Music**: Upbeat but minimal bedroom-pop or light funk, nothing corporate anthem.
- **Key stat for text overlay**: 48% open rate vs ~15–20% industry average
