## Executive Summary

Gmail's Promotions tab is inbox organization, not a deliverability penalty. Emails placed in Promotions are fully delivered and visible to users — they're categorized as commercial rather than personal. This distinction matters because many senders misread Promotions placement as a reputation problem when it's a sorting preference.

Gmail classifies email using four primary signals: content patterns (promotional language, discount codes), sender reputation (engagement history, authentication via SPF/DKIM/DMARC), recipient behavior (opens, replies, individual engagement patterns), and email structure (HTML density, link count, image-to-text ratio). Of these, recipient behavior carries the most weight. Classification is personalized — the same email lands differently for different users based on their individual history with the sender.

Newsletters inherently trigger every commercial email signal: bulk sending, scheduled delivery, consistent templates, broad audiences. Promotions placement for newsletters is Gmail's system working as designed, not a failure.

TLDR's flagship newsletter maintains a 48% open rate across 7M+ subscribers despite landing in Promotions for most Gmail users. The industry average open rate is 15-20%. This performance gap demonstrates that tab placement is not the primary driver of engagement — subscriber trust and content quality are.

The most effective countermeasure is direct subscriber action. When a user drags an email from Promotions to Primary, Gmail prompts confirmation and permanently reclassifies future emails from that sender for that user. Secondary measures include minimal HTML formatting, reduced link density, plain-text email versions, and consistent sending schedules. None guarantee Primary placement, but all improve deliverability signals.

The critical distinction for any email program: Promotions means commercial classification with full delivery. Spam means Gmail considers the email unwanted — users never see it, and sender reputation takes lasting damage. Conflating the two leads to misallocated effort on the wrong problem.
