# Video Script: State of Newsletters 2026 — What the Report Actually Says

**Format:** Talking head / explainer

## Hook (0:00-0:15)

28 billion emails. 255 million unique readers. Open rates climbing. The 2026 State of Newsletters report just dropped, and the headline is clear: newsletters aren't just surviving — they're pulling away from every other owned-media format.

Here's what the report actually says.

## Section 1: Email Beats the Algorithm (0:15-0:50)

Social reach is a moving target. One algorithm change can cut your audience overnight. Email is different — when someone subscribes, they're opting into a direct relationship.

That's why newsletters consistently outperform social on engagement. TLDR hits 48% open rates. Industry average is 15-20%. The gap comes from editorial curation, consistent scheduling, and respecting readers' time.

The report confirms this pattern across the entire ecosystem. Newsletters that deliver clear value on a reliable schedule earn attention that social platforms can't match.

## Section 2: Growth Is Multi-Channel (0:50-1:30)

No single channel scales forever. The report shows the newsletters growing fastest are running paid acquisition, referral programs, and SEO simultaneously.

TLDR grew to 7 million subscribers across 12 newsletters by treating growth as a multi-channel operation. Paid for initial scale. Referrals for compounding organic growth. SEO for steady long-term traffic.

If you're betting everything on one channel, the report says you're leaving growth on the table.

## Section 3: The Ad Market Is Maturing (1:30-2:15)

Advertisers are moving budget from spray-and-pray display to high-attention newsletter environments. Premium placements, native formats, and editorial voice command higher rates because they work better.

The report shows that newsletters offering copywriting services and performance reporting as part of the ad package win more repeat business. TLDR includes both at no extra cost.

The proof: Delve saw 52x ROI through TLDR ads. Redact got 50% lower CPC than LinkedIn. When readers actually pay attention, advertisers get results.

## Section 4: Revenue Diversification (2:15-2:50)

Paid subscription revenue jumped significantly year-over-year. The growth is concentrated in niche creators delivering specialized expertise.

But the smartest move isn't choosing between ads and subscriptions — it's running both. Free content builds the audience. Paid tiers monetize the most engaged segment. The two models reinforce each other.

The report also highlights events, podcasts, and communities as emerging revenue streams. Multi-format newsletters retain subscribers longer because there are more reasons to stay engaged.

## Section 5: AI Changes Operations, Not the Product (2:50-3:20)

AI is reshaping how newsletters get produced. Content curation, subject line testing, audience segmentation, personalized delivery — the operational layer is getting automated.

But here's the nuance the report misses: readers subscribe for editorial judgment. Automated curation without human voice produces generic content. The newsletters winning in 2026 use AI as tooling, not as the voice.

## Closing (3:20-3:40)

The report's core finding: engagement quality, not subscriber count, is what separates the newsletters pulling ahead. Track the metrics that matter, diversify revenue, and build beyond the inbox.

The newsletter is the foundation. Everything else is the structure you build on top.
