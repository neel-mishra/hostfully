## Hook (0:00-0:15)

Paid acquisition is getting more expensive every quarter. Organic social reach keeps shrinking. But there's a growth channel that costs zero dollars and consistently delivers subscribers who actually open your emails.

Newsletter swaps. Here's how to do them right.

## Section 1: Why Trust Transfers (0:15-0:45)

The core mechanism is simple. When a reader's favorite newsletter recommends yours, that recommendation carries more weight than any Facebook ad.

These subscribers arrive with intent, not curiosity. They already read newsletters. They already trust the source that sent them. So they open at higher rates, retain longer, and engage more with everything downstream.

The economics: zero ad spend, minimal setup time, and higher-quality subscribers than paid channels. For most newsletters, this is the best available ROI.

## Section 2: The Three Mistakes That Kill Swaps (0:45-1:45)

Most failed swaps share three problems.

Mistake one — shallow audience matching. "We're both marketing newsletters" isn't enough. A B2B enterprise newsletter and a freelance tips newsletter share a category but almost zero audience overlap. Dig into demographics, job titles, content focus. If the readers wouldn't naturally subscribe to both, the swap won't convert.

Mistake two — over-promotion. Blasting a partner's audience with multiple emails kills trust. One well-placed mention or one dedicated send. That's the right dose.

Mistake three — no onboarding. Someone subscribes through a swap, then gets silence for a week. By the time your next issue arrives, they've forgotten why they signed up. A welcome email within minutes is non-negotiable.

And a bonus mistake — no tracking. If you can't attribute subscribers to a specific swap, you can't tell which partnerships are worth repeating.

## Section 3: Three Formats That Work (1:45-2:30)

Format one — dedicated email swaps. Your partner sends their full list a recommendation of your newsletter. You reciprocate. Highest impact, but use sparingly. Audience fatigue is real.

Format two — in-content mentions. A paragraph or "recommended reads" section inside a regular issue. Lower conversion per mention, but lower friction. Great for ongoing partnerships.

Format three — multi-partner swap chains. Three or more newsletters promote each other in sequence. Harder to coordinate, but the compounding reach across audiences accelerates growth.

## Section 4: Finding Partners and Writing Copy (2:30-3:30)

Start with your own subscribers. Survey them. Ask what else they read. Your ideal swap partners are in the answers.

Look for complementary niches, not competitors. A marketing automation newsletter and a sales enablement newsletter serve overlapping audiences with different content. That's the sweet spot.

For the copy: lead with the benefit, not the description. Not "we cover AI and startups" — instead, "the 5-minute briefing your CTO already reads." Match the partner's voice. One CTA. One link.

## Section 5: Measuring What Matters (3:30-4:00)

Track three things: signups attributed to the swap, 30-day retention, and engagement rates compared to your average.

The critical metric isn't how many people signed up. It's how many are still opening a month later. If swap subscribers aren't engaging after 30 days, the audience match was wrong.

## Closing (4:00-4:15)

Build a roster of 5-10 reliable partners. Rotate monthly. Track retention, not just signups. Newsletter swaps aren't a hack — they're infrastructure.
